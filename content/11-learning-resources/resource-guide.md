The best resource is the one assigned to a real musical problem. Do not buy or bookmark everything at once.

## Best first purchase: Creative Guitar 1

**Guthrie Govan — Creative Guitar 1: Cutting-Edge Techniques**
Level: developing to advanced guitarist
Cost: paid book
Best for: building the theory/technique/musical-thinking base directly from Guthrie's teaching.
Use: read a small section weekly; sing/play every concept and use it in that week's improvisation.
Caveat: confirm whether the edition supplies downloadable audio or a CD.
Official page: https://www.halleonard.com/product/14007762/creative-guitar-1

## Second purchase after foundations: Creative Guitar 2

**Guthrie Govan — Creative Guitar 2: Advanced Techniques**
Level: advanced
Cost: paid book
Best for: adding advanced mechanics/articulation after Book 1 concepts work musically.
Use: begin around weeks 9–13, not as a replacement for ear/rhythm work.
Caveat: difficult examples invite tension if tempo becomes the only score.
Official page: https://www.halleonard.com/product/14007763/creative-guitar-2

## Best selective premium source

**JTC Guitar — Guthrie Govan artist catalogue**
Level: intermediate to elite, product-dependent
Cost: paid
Best for: official lessons, notation and backing material for a carefully chosen study.
Use: buy one relevant product after attempting the target by ear; study one phrase deeply.
Caveat: avoid collecting courses faster than you apply them.
Link: https://jtcguitar.com/store/artist/guthrie-govan

## Best free Guthrie principles

**Guthrie's ten improvisation tips**
Level: all
Cost: free
Best for: phrases as speech, ear-to-fretboard connection, risk, one-string playing, musical theory use.
Use: take one tip per week and build a recorded constraint around it.
Link: https://guitar.com/features/10-tips-guthrie-govan/

**Guthrie's string-bending masterclass**
Level: intermediate+
Cost: free article/video where available
Best for: pitch-aware bends, pre-bends, releases, muting and systematic pentatonic exploration.
Use: compare against target notes; never force painful wide bends.
Link: https://www.musicradar.com/tuition/guitars/guthrie-govans-string-bending-masterclass-part-1-628750

## Best free theory spine

**Open Music Theory**
Level: fundamentals through advanced academic topics
Cost: free/open
Best for: intervals, scales, triads, sevenths, symbols, voice leading and pop/rock harmony.
Use: look up the concept, then spell/sing/build it on guitar and use it over the offline backing player.
Caveat: some strict classical rules are style-specific, not universal laws.
Start: https://openmusictheory.github.io/contents.html

**musictheory.net exercises**
Level: fundamentals/intermediate
Cost: free web exercises; optional paid app
Best for: rapid visual note, interval, scale and chord identification/construction.
Use: 5–10 minutes, then repeat the answers by ear/on guitar.
Caveat: visual success alone is not ear training.
Link: https://www.musictheory.net/exercises

## Best practice-science takeaways

- Interleave related tasks instead of repeating one item mindlessly for an hour: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27588014/
- Spread work across days; one marathon does not replace consistent exposure: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15551084/
- Combine imagining, singing and physical playing: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8573425/
- On long sessions, use regular breaks and track total load: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4086404/

## What not to buy yet

- giant lick libraries with no listening/application system;
- “secret speed” programs promising fixed BPM gains quickly;
- another advanced course while unfinished courses remain unused;
- software that guesses chords when you have not first attempted the bass and quality by ear.

Your built-in offline dashboard already supplies original synchronized backing progressions, chord construction, constraints, timing and tracking.
