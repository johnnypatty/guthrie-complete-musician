Your voice is not found by avoiding influences. It appears when influences pass through your ears, taste, limitations, experiences, and repeated choices.

## Influence map

Create five columns:

- Guthrie: phrasing, rhythm, harmony, playfulness;
- Andy Timmons/Gary Moore/Jeff Beck: emotional timing and articulation;
- Yngwie/Becker/Gilbert/Vai/Van Halen: technical and sonic vocabulary;
- metal favourites: rhythm, intensity, form, dissonance;
- non-guitar music: melody, groove, harmony, production, humour.

Under each, write principles—not “sound exactly like.”

## Three-variation rule

Nothing enters your vocabulary until you can alter rhythm, harmony/key, and articulation/register. Then combine the principle with an idea from a different column.

## Monthly original statement

Record 60 seconds with:

- one memorable motif;
- one rhythmic identity;
- one harmonic color you chose;
- one expressive technique;
- one surprise;
- a clear ending.

Do not explain it before listening. Afterward, label which choices feel natural and which feel borrowed/forced.

## Taste journal

When a moment makes you laugh, rewind, or feel something, write the timestamp and *why*. “Fast” is insufficient. Was it timing, contrast, note choice, tone, recovery, exaggeration, silence, or expectation?

## Success test

After several months, recordings reveal recurring choices you did not deliberately copy, and you can refine rather than erase them.
