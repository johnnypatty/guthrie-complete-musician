This is the expressive center of the system. Accuracy without character is unfinished; character without pitch control is unreliable.

## Pitch-aware bends

### Purpose

Reach and leave target pitches intentionally.

### Method

1. Play the target fretted note.
2. Play the lower starting note.
3. Bend to the remembered target and hold it.
4. Compare against the target again.
5. Pre-bend silently, pick at the target, then release in time.

Practise semitone, whole-tone, and only physically comfortable wider bends. Explore small blues inflections on suitable scale degrees. Support bends with neighboring fingers and mute the string at the desired endpoint when the phrase should not audibly return.

### Success test

Five random target bends land near the intended pitch without searching, remain stable for two beats, and release/mute as planned.

### Common mistake

Judging pitch by hand position. A correct bend is heard, not seen.

## Vibrato controls

Treat vibrato as three independent choices:

- **Onset:** immediate or delayed;
- **Rate:** slow, medium, fast;
- **Width:** narrow, medium, wide.

Play the same note nine ways by combining rate and width, then make three musical versions with different onset. Keep the pitch center stable.

## Dynamics and pick attack

Play one phrase at whisper, conversation, and shout levels without changing tempo. Repeat nearer bridge/neck and with pick angle/depth changes. Record level-matched versions so “louder” is not mistaken for “better.”

## Harmonics

Map natural harmonics, then practise one artificial/pinched harmonic as a controlled articulation. Find the picking-hand node rather than increasing gain. Use harmonics as phrase punctuation, not constant decoration.

## Weekly application

Take one Gary Moore, Jeff Beck, Andy Timmons, Guthrie, or Vai phrase by ear. Identify every bend target, vibrato onset, slide, dynamic accent, and harmonic. Create a response using different notes but the same expressive logic.
