## Purpose

Create connected notes with deliberate rhythm and equal musical authority—not merely softer attacks between picked notes.

## Method

### Hammer-on balance

Play a picked target note repeatedly, then match a hammered note to its volume without slamming. Start with two fingers and change combinations. Keep fingertip travel small.

### Pull-off direction

Release slightly toward the floor/palm so the lower note speaks, but avoid pulling the string sharp. Listen for pitch bend and adjacent-string noise.

### One-pick phrases

Pick only the first note of a three- or four-note fragment. Apply accents that do not coincide with the picked note. This exposes whether rhythm comes from the ear or the pick.

### Position-shift legato

Connect two short shapes with slide, hammer, or picked articulation. Practise the connection alone, then choose the version whose sound fits the phrase.

### Tapping as an added finger

Begin with one tapped note that completes a chord or arpeggio. Mute lower strings with the picking-hand palm/unused fingers. Then add tapped slides or multiple right-hand fingers only when the single-note rhythm is stable.

### Musical application

Over one chord, create three lines using identical pitches:

1. mostly picked and percussive;
2. flowing legato;
3. legato with one tapped extension.

Judge which articulation tells the intended story.

## Success test

Recorded notes have even rhythmic placement, intentional volume differences, stable pitch, and no background ringing. The phrase remains recognizable at quiet dynamics.

## Common mistake

Using compression/gain to make uneven hammer-ons sound equal. Practise clean first; compression is production, not coordination.
