These are short cultural “sentences.” Learn only the recognizable fragment first, in any comfortable key, with no tab. Then find it in two more neck areas and create one variation. Song/theme names are study targets; notation and lyrics are intentionally absent.

## Level 1 — Everyone knows the contour

| Target | Main lesson | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Happy Birthday | intervals and phrase endings | begin on three different strings |
| Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star | repeated notes and perfect fifth | sing degrees, play two keys |
| Ode to Joy | stepwise melody and rhythm | play from memory without searching |
| Beethoven's Fifth opening | short-short-short-long rhythm | move motif through three pitch levels |
| Für Elise opening | chromatic neighbors | keep rhythm/contour recognizable |
| Jingle Bells hook | repeated-note rhythm | accents remain clear at two tempos |
| Nokia ringtone | contour and leaps | find without watching a performance |
| Happy Birthday bass/chords | hearing root movement | support your own melody version |

## Level 2 — Film, television, and game identity

| Target | Main lesson | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Super Mario main-theme opening | rhythm and articulation | clap first, then play accurately |
| Super Mario underground motif | chromatic pattern | transpose by ear |
| The Simpsons opening interval | Lydian color | identify the surprising interval |
| Pink Panther theme | chromatic phrasing | preserve swing/space |
| James Bond motif | minor color and rhythm | build a short ending |
| Mission: Impossible theme | 5-feel and accents | count/group without losing pulse |
| Star Wars main-theme opening | large melodic leaps | sing every leap before playing |
| Pirates of the Caribbean theme fragment | repeated rhythmic cells | maintain accents under speed |
| The Godfather theme | expressive minor melody | add deliberate slides/vibrato |
| Fallen Down | game melody and voice leading | arrange melody plus bass note |
| E1M1/Hangar riff identity | chromatic metal rhythm | mute tightly at two tempos |
| SpongeBob Grass Skirt Chase fragment | rhythmic character | preserve bounce, not just notes |

## Level 3 — Riffs and bass lines

| Target | Main lesson | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Seven Nation Army | scale-degree hearing | play in three keys |
| Smoke on the Water | fourths and rhythm | identify exact rests/lengths |
| Iron Man | bends/slides and space | match articulation without tab |
| Another One Bites the Dust | bass pulse | lock with a drum loop |
| Billie Jean bass line | repetition and consistency | one minute with stable accents |
| Sunshine of Your Love | blues color | explain major/minor mixture |
| Day Tripper | syncopated riff | sing bass contour, then play |
| Enter Sandman opening | clean riff timing | map repeated/open-string relationship |
| For Whom the Bell Tolls opening | phrase placement | enter correctly after a count-in |
| Beat It rhythm-guitar hook | groove and muting | record clean and gained versions |
| Sweet Child O' Mine opening idea | sequence and string crossing | find structure rather than memorize digits |
| Redbone main guitar/bass idea | pocket and articulation | play behind/center/ahead, choose best |

## Level 4 — Expressive lead fragments

| Target | Main lesson | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Still Got the Blues main melody | target bends and vibrato | tune bends against target notes |
| Parisienne Walkways sustained phrase | vibrato, sustain, restraint | make one long note tell a story |
| Comfortably Numb first-solo opening | melodic pacing | preserve rests and bend pitch |
| Marooned opening phrases | whammy/bend-like vocal contour | sing contour and make your articulation choice |
| Cause We've Ended as Lovers theme | touch and micro-dynamics | three level-matched expressive takes |
| Cry for You main-theme fragment | motif and development | create a response in another key |
| The Loner melody fragment | legato and sustain | play cleanly with less gain |
| November Rain first-solo opening | chord-tone melody | name landing notes over chords |

## The rule

Do not race through the list. A fully heard five-second melody is worth more than ten half-guessed riffs. Choose one target per week alongside your personal repertoire project.
