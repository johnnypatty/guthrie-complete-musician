## Purpose

Feel unusual groupings as phrases rather than perform arithmetic while playing.

## Method

### Group before counting

- 5/4: feel 3+2 and 2+3.
- 7/8: feel 2+2+3, 3+2+2, and 2+3+2.
- Nine-note line over 4/4: keep the bar pulse while the phrase start moves.

Clap the group accents, speak a short phrase matching the grouping, then play one note. Add pitches only after the cycle is bodily clear.

### Constant riff, changing accents

Play a repeating note/fret cell while accents group 3, 5, or 7 sixteenths against 4/4. Mark the bar's beat one with a foot tap. Notice when the grouping realigns.

### Constant harmony, changing phrase length

Over a four-bar track, make a three-beat motif followed by a one-beat rest. Next use five-beat phrases. The backing form does not wait for you; resolve deliberately when harmony changes.

### Repertoire application

Use small sections from Fives, Dream Theater/Liquid Tension Experiment, Periphery, or technical-metal favourites. First identify the grouping by ear, then verify the full bar count. Do not learn only the fretting pattern.

## Success test

You can count the large pulse, clap accents, play the riff, stop for one cycle, and re-enter without the recording rescuing you.

## Common mistake

Calling any awkward riff “odd meter.” It may be syncopation or an odd grouping inside ordinary 4/4; identify both the phrase and the bar.
