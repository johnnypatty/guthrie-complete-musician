## Purpose

Develop the percussive control needed for Wonderful Slippery Thing-style grooves, clean funk, neo-soul, and rhythmically alive lead playing.

## Method

### Continuous motion

Keep the picking hand moving in sixteenths while the fretting hand decides which attacks become pitches, muted scratches, ties, or silence. Start with one chord fragment.

### Accent grid

Over one bar of sixteenths, accent:

1. each beat;
2. only the “e” positions;
3. only the “and” positions;
4. only the “a” positions;
5. a chosen syncopated two-accent pattern.

The unaccented strokes must remain quieter, not disappear mechanically.

### Chord fragments

Use two or three upper notes rather than full six-string shapes. Move between two inversions with the smallest available motion. Release fretting pressure for short notes without lifting far enough to lose the groove.

### Touch-wah awareness

Practise once without envelope/wah, then with it. The filter reacts to attack, so inconsistent picking changes both volume and vowel. Control the groove acoustically before relying on the effect.

### Lead/rhythm exchange

Play two bars of groove, answer with a one-bar lead phrase, leave one bar of rhythm. Never let the lead phrase erase the sixteenth-note grid in your body.

## Success test

One unedited minute contains clear accents, intentional scratches, short clean chord fragments, stable tempo, and a seamless return after lead fills.

## Common mistake

Muting by squeezing harder. Funk muting often comes from precise pressure release and coordinated unused-string contact.
