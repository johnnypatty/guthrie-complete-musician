Take this once before week 1. Save every raw recording in a folder dated `Baseline`. Do not rehearse first; the purpose is an honest starting point.

## 1. Time and groove

- Record 60 seconds of muted sixteenth-note funk at a comfortable tempo.
- Record 60 seconds of eighth-note rhythm with the click only on beats 2 and 4.
- Score 0–5: pulse stability, consistent subdivision, accents, muting, recovery.

## 2. Clean control

- Choose one alternate-picked three-note-per-string sequence and one legato sequence you already know.
- Find a tempo where two complete takes are clean with tension no higher than 3/10.
- Write the tempo; do not compare the two techniques competitively.

## 3. Bends and vibrato

- Play the target note, then bend to it from a semitone and a whole tone below.
- Record a sustained note with narrow, medium, and wide vibrato.
- Score 0–5: pitch center, arrival, release, muting, deliberate width/rate.

## 4. Ear

- Choose an unfamiliar but simple 5–10 second melody.
- Give yourself 15 minutes with no tab/video fingering.
- Record the result and note how much you could sing, identify, and play.

## 5. Fretboard and harmony

- Randomly choose 12 natural notes; find each on a named string within five seconds.
- Spell Cmaj7, Cm7, C7, Cm7b5, F#m7, Bbmaj7, and E7b9.
- Build three inversions of one major and one minor triad on adjacent string sets.

## 6. Improvisation

Use one backing track and record three unedited 90-second takes:

1. free;
2. maximum four notes plus rests;
3. target each chord's third on a strong beat.

Score 0–5: rhythm, motif, harmony, dynamics, articulation, form, risk/recovery.

## 7. Communication

Record a 30-second spoken introduction and then play one familiar piece without restarting.

## Keep these results

Do not reduce the baseline to one number. Circle the weakest *controllable behavior* in each category. Those become week 1 goals.
