Add these **after** the core. Each block is 30–45 minutes and must have one deliverable. Rest 10–15 minutes after 45–60 minutes of playing. A five-hour day should contain listening, writing, singing, and review—not five hours of maximum-speed fretting.

## A. Repertoire project

- Recall a known section cold.
- Repair one transition.
- Map the next short section by ear.
- Record a complete take.
- Deliverable: one clearly named recording plus an updated repertoire tracker.

## B. Deep transcription

- Listen away from the guitar and mark form.
- Sing and map bass movement.
- Transcribe a lead phrase in tiny loops.
- Write degrees/rhythm/articulation.
- Create three variations.
- Deliverable: completed Transcription Worksheet.

## C. Composition and arrangement

- Choose one progression or famous melody.
- Create two contrasting sections.
- Assign register, dynamics, and rhythmic roles.
- Make a 60–90 second demo.
- Deliverable: demo with a written form map.

## D. Tone and recording

- Record the same phrase with three gain/EQ/ambience choices.
- Level-match before judging.
- Test noise, pick attack, muting, and backing-track balance.
- Deliverable: one chosen tone plus two sentences explaining why.

## E. Performance and communication

- Give a 30–60 second introduction.
- Play the piece without restarting.
- Recover musically from mistakes.
- Watch once for music and once for body language/speaking.
- Deliverable: one uncut performance video and three observations.

## A safe five-hour example

1. 90-minute core
2. 15-minute break
3. 45-minute repertoire project
4. meal/walk away from the instrument
5. 45-minute transcription (only part requires continuous guitar)
6. 15-minute break
7. 45-minute composition/arrangement
8. long break
9. 45-minute performance/recording
10. 15-minute review and planning

Never increase from a quiet week directly to repeated five-hour days. Track total playing load, not just formal practice.
