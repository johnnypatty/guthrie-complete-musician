This is the recommended serious day. It keeps the complete 90-minute core and adds repertoire plus deep phrase study.

| Time | Block | Outcome |
|---:|---|---|
| 5 min | Prepare | Tune, relax, state goals |
| 20 min | Clean technique | Measured mechanic plus musical application |
| 15 min | Rhythm and groove | Stable form and subdivision |
| 15 min | Ear/transcription | One small no-tabs fragment |
| 15 min | Fretboard/harmony | Spell, hear, locate, apply |
| 15 min | Improvisation | Three recorded constrained takes |
| 15 min | Repertoire | Join and polish an already-mapped section |
| 15 min | Phrase laboratory | Analyze and transform one expressive idea |
| 5 min | Listen and log | One win and one next action |
| **120 min** |  |  |

## Repertoire block

Do not spend all 15 minutes discovering new notes. Split it:

- 3 minutes: recall yesterday's section without audio.
- 5 minutes: repair the weakest transition at a manageable tempo.
- 4 minutes: play with the recording/backing and preserve articulation.
- 3 minutes: perform the whole known section without stopping.

**Success test:** the section works from a cold start twice and keeps its form after one small mistake.

## Phrase laboratory

Choose one phrase from your ear work or repertoire—not necessarily Guthrie. Identify rhythm, chord relationship, contour, articulation, and emotional effect. Then make:

1. a rhythmic variation with the pitches mostly unchanged;
2. a melodic variation preserving the rhythm;
3. a version moved to another key or chord quality;
4. a response phrase in your own voice.

**Success test:** the fourth version no longer sounds like a copied exercise but preserves the useful principle.

## If you only have 60 minutes

Do not practise technique for the entire hour. Use 5 prepare, 15 technique, 10 rhythm, 10 ear, 10 harmony, 10 improvisation. Resume repertoire tomorrow.
