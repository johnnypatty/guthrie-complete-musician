Use this on ordinary days. Keep transitions short and use a timer.

## 0:00–0:05 — Prepare (5 minutes)

**Purpose:** arrive physically and mentally before demanding repetition.

**Method:** tune; play quiet open-string and chromatic movements; check shoulders, jaw, thumb pressure, breathing, pick grip, and unwanted string noise. Choose today's exact goals.

**Success test:** the guitar is in tune, hands feel warm rather than tired, and goals are written in one sentence.

**Common mistake:** calling fast scale runs a warm-up. Speed is training load, not preparation.

## 0:05–0:25 — Clean technique (20 minutes)

Choose one primary mechanic for the week and one five-minute maintenance mechanic. Work in this order:

1. two minutes of slow motion with perfect muting;
2. six minutes of short clean bursts;
3. six minutes of connected musical fragments;
4. four minutes at a sustainable tempo;
5. two minutes applied to a phrase or improvised line.

**Success test:** two clean recorded repetitions with consistent rhythm and tension no higher than 3/10.

**Common mistake:** raising BPM because one lucky repetition worked.

## 0:25–0:40 — Rhythm and groove (15 minutes)

Clap/count first, then guitar. Rotate: subdivisions, click on 2/4, sparse click, accent displacement, funk muting, odd-meter grouping, rhythm/lead switching.

**Success test:** one minute without losing the form; playback sounds settled rather than chased.

**Common mistake:** evaluating only whether the correct notes occurred.

## 0:40–0:55 — Ear and transcription (15 minutes)

Use the No-Tabs Method. Sing a fragment, locate the pulse/tonal center, then map only 1–4 seconds. On easier days, transcribe bass or chord quality instead of lead guitar.

**Success test:** sing and play today's fragment from memory, then state its first note as a scale degree or interval from the tonal center.

**Common mistake:** touching the guitar before the phrase can be sung.

## 0:55–1:10 — Fretboard, harmony, and chord creation (15 minutes)

Take one concept—target note, triad, seventh chord, inversion, extension, mode—and do four things: spell it, sing key tones, find it in three neck areas, use it over a progression.

**Success test:** answer one random note/chord question in five seconds, then make a musical two-chord or two-bar example.

**Common mistake:** memorizing a grip without knowing its notes or function.

## 1:10–1:25 — Improvisation (15 minutes)

Use one constraint: one string, three notes, rhythm only, motif and three variations, chord third on beat one, rests after every phrase, dynamic arc, or inside/outside resolution.

Record three takes:

- Take 1: explore.
- Take 2: develop the best idea from Take 1.
- Take 3: perform a beginning, middle, and ending.

**Success test:** identify one memorable motif and one moment that responded to the backing.

**Common mistake:** restarting every time a risky idea fails.

## 1:25–1:30 — Listen and log (5 minutes)

Listen without playing. Record: minutes, clean tempo, one win, one problem, one exact next action. Save the best and most revealing takes—not only the flattering one.

**Total: 90 minutes.**
