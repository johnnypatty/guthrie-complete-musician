Keep one main technical focus for a week while changing its musical application. This gives repetition without mindless sameness.

| Day | Emphasis | Core variation |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Baseline and precision | Choose weekly technique; record starting tempo and groove |
| Tuesday | Ear and melody | Longer singing/transcription; improvise on one string |
| Wednesday | Harmony and rhythm | Triads/voice leading; click on 2/4 or sparse click |
| Thursday | Speed and vocabulary | Short bursts; transform one phrase into three contexts |
| Friday | Repertoire | Join sections; perform without restarting |
| Saturday | Creative long day | Optional composition, full cover, jam, or video project |
| Sunday | Light review | 30–60 minutes: listen, gentle technique, assess, plan; no speed test |

## Weekly rules

- Technique tempo is tested Monday and Friday only. Other days build quality.
- Transcription occurs at least four days.
- Improvisation is recorded at least three days.
- One session each week uses a clean tone; gain must not hide noise or weak articulation.
- One session starts from an unfamiliar key or neck area.
- One complete take is kept even when imperfect.

## Four-week cycle

- Week A: establish movement and concept.
- Week B: connect positions/string sets and vary rhythm.
- Week C: apply to repertoire and improvisation.
- Week D: reduce physical load, record tests, and decide what transfers forward.

Do not carry every old exercise forever. Keep a short maintenance set and replace solved drills with real musical problems.
