Improvisation becomes understandable when phrases behave like speech: ideas, breath, emphasis, response, development, and conclusion.

## Purpose

Replace continuous scale motion with intentional musical statements.

## Method

### One breath

Sing a phrase in one breath, then play it. Leave an equal or longer silence. Repeat with a response.

### Punctuation

- comma: short rest, idea continues;
- question: unresolved rhythm/pitch;
- answer: stronger resolution;
- full stop: longer rest or clear ending.

Record 60 seconds containing all four.

### Important word

Choose one note as the sentence's important word. Reach it through rhythm, register, bend, dynamic accent, or harmonic tension. Do not make every note important.

### Beginning, middle, end

For a 90-second take:

- first 30 seconds introduce a small idea;
- middle develops register/rhythm/intensity;
- final section simplifies and creates an ending.

## Success test

On playback, you can hum at least one phrase and point to deliberate silence, development, climax, and ending.

## Common mistake

Calling random pauses “phrasing.” The material before and after a rest must relate.
