Outside playing works when the listener can hear where “inside” is and believes you meant to leave and return.

## Tension ladder

1. chord tones;
2. diatonic non-chord tones;
3. blues inflection/chromatic approach;
4. altered extensions over a dominant;
5. side-slip a phrase by a semitone;
6. short symmetrical/remote shape;
7. clear resolution to a meaningful target.

Do not begin at level 6 if levels 1–3 are not musical.

## Method

### One wrong note made right

Choose a chromatic note outside the current scale. Hear its nearest resolutions. Place it weak and resolve immediately; then hold it longer, accent it, sequence it, and still resolve convincingly.

### Side-slip with identity

State a motif inside. Move the motif up/down one semitone briefly, then return on a strong chord tone. Because rhythm/contour remain recognizable, the detour sounds intentional.

### Dominant alteration

Over V7 resolving to i/I, sing 3rd and b7, then add b9/#9/b13 as directional tension. Track exactly where each note resolves.

### Rhythm before pitch

An outside phrase with confident placement can work; an uncertain one sounds accidental. Clap the phrase, then choose pitches.

## Success test

Make three versions—mild, medium, strong tension—and the next-day listener can identify the release point in each.

## Common mistake

Moving a memorized shape randomly and calling every collision “fusion.” Resolution, rhythm, and context create the effect.
