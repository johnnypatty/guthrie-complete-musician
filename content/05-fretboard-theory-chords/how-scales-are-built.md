## The chromatic ruler

Western equal-tempered music divides the octave into 12 semitones. A scale is a chosen collection of those distances from a root. Learn formulas as intervals and sounds, then discover fingerings.

## Major scale

Formula: **1 2 3 4 5 6 7**
Step pattern: whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half.

Example: C major = C D E F G A B. Build G major by applying the same distances: G A B C D E F#.

## Useful families

| Sound | Formula |
|---|---|
| Major pentatonic | 1 2 3 5 6 |
| Minor pentatonic | 1 b3 4 5 b7 |
| Blues | 1 b3 4 b5 5 b7 |
| Natural minor | 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7 |
| Harmonic minor | 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 7 |
| Melodic minor (ascending/jazz use) | 1 2 b3 4 5 6 7 |
| Whole tone | 1 2 3 #4 #5 b7 |
| Diminished half-whole | 1 b2 b3 3 #4 5 6 b7 |

## Seven modes of major

Do not learn modes as “major scale starting from another fret.” Hear each against its own tonal center:

- Ionian: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
- Dorian: 1 2 b3 4 5 6 b7 — minor with natural 6
- Phrygian: 1 b2 b3 4 5 b6 b7 — minor with b2
- Lydian: 1 2 3 #4 5 6 7 — major with #4
- Mixolydian: 1 2 3 4 5 6 b7 — major with b7
- Aeolian: 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7
- Locrian: 1 b2 b3 4 b5 b6 b7

## Seven-step learning method

1. Spell the scale in one key.
2. Sing root and characteristic note.
3. Play on one string.
4. Find in one compact area.
5. Build its diatonic triads/sevenths.
6. Improvise with three notes and rests.
7. Compose a phrase that clearly features the characteristic degree.

## Success test

Someone can hold a root drone and you make Dorian, Lydian, or Mixolydian recognizable with a short musical statement—not by reciting a full scale.
