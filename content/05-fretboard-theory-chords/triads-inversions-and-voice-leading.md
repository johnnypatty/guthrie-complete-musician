## Purpose

Turn the whole neck into small movable harmony and create smooth chord movement useful for funk, neo-soul, fusion, accompaniment, and melodic soloing.

## Inversions

For a C major triad (C E G):

- root position has C in bass;
- first inversion has E in bass;
- second inversion has G in bass.

The chord quality remains C major. An inversion changes bass/register and voice-leading behavior.

## String-set method

Work one quality and key through:

- strings 1–3;
- strings 2–4;
- strings 3–5;
- strings 4–6.

On each set, find the three inversions by note names. Say root/third/fifth aloud. Repeat for minor, diminished, and augmented.

## Voice-leading rules as creative tools

1. Keep common tones when useful.
2. Move remaining voices by the smallest distance.
3. Prefer contrary or oblique motion when every voice moving together sounds clumsy.
4. Treat the highest note as a melody.
5. Break the rule deliberately when a leap creates the desired dramatic register change.

## Progression laboratory

Take Fmaj7–A7–Dm7–Bbmaj7. Reduce each chord to an upper triad/essential three-note grip. Create:

1. nearest-motion version;
2. ascending top-melody version;
3. descending top-melody version;
4. syncopated funk version with short notes.

Then improvise using only the notes of each current triad. Connect with one chromatic approach into the next chord.

## Success test

You can play I–vi–ii–V or another four-chord progression in three neck areas without jumping to four unrelated root-position shapes, and can sing the top line.

## Common mistake

Calling a movable grip an inversion without knowing which chord member is in the bass.
