Rest and Injury Prevention

This is preventive guidance, not medical diagnosis.

Progressive daily-practice • rest • injury • prevention

This is preventive guidance, not medical diagnosis.

The non-negotiable stop signs

Stop playing for pain, burning, numbness, tingling, unusual weakness, swelling, or loss of coordination. Do not “play through” these signals. If symptoms persist, recur, or affect normal activity, tell a parent/guardian and consult a qualified healthcare professional familiar with musicians.

Long-session rules

  • Take 10–15 minutes away from the instrument after 45–60 minutes.
  • Difficult, high-force repertoire needs more frequent breaks.
  • After vacation or illness, rebuild duration and speed over several days.
  • Count rehearsals, jamming, and casual playing in the day's total load.
  • Use mental rehearsal, singing, score/form study, and listening when the hands need rest.

Efficient posture checks

  • Guitar position allows both wrists to remain reasonably neutral.
  • Shoulders stay down; jaw and breath remain free.
  • Fretting thumb supplies only the pressure needed for a clean note.
  • Pick grip does not tighten as tempo rises.
  • Chair/strap position stays similar between seated and standing practice.

Tension scale

Log perceived tension from 0–10. Most technical work should remain 0–3. At 4, slow down and shorten the passage. At 5 or more, stop the attempt and reset. A tempo that requires bracing the whole body is not owned.

Hearing safety

Use the quietest headphone/monitor level that reveals detail. High frequencies that feel exciting for a minute can fatigue hearing and make tone judgment worse. Rest your ears before comparing EQ or ambience.
