Do not refret by calendar

Playing for one year does not automatically mean the frets need replacement. Inspect actual wear and playability. The original j.custom nickel-silver fretwork is valuable and may last for years depending on touch, strings, bending, maintenance, and total use.

Consider work when

  • visible grooves/flat spots create intonation or feel problems;
  • bends choke despite a correct neck/setup;
  • repeated buzzing cannot be solved with reasonable setup;
  • a level/crown would leave frets lower than you want;
  • or you strongly prefer stainless feel and accept modification cost/originality impact.

Often a skilled level/crown/polish extends the original frets. Get the diagnosis before requesting replacement.

Why this requires the right luthier

The neck is bound and the factory fret ends receive high-grade j.custom treatment. Stainless wire is harder on tools and mistakes can chip the board/binding, alter radius, create high frets, or produce rough ends. Use a luthier with documented stainless refrets on bound high-end Ibanez necks—not simply a shop that says it can install frets.

Questions for a quote

  1. How many stainless refrets have you completed on bound Ibanez/Japanese superstrat necks?
  2. How will you protect binding and preserve/refinish the rounded fret ends?
  3. Which wire dimensions/alloy would match or intentionally change the existing jumbo feel?
  4. Is board levelling, nut work, full Lo-Pro Edge setup, intonation and pickup-height check included?
  5. What happens if the locking nut height must change?
  6. Can you show close photographs of comparable completed work?
  7. What is the total itemized price and turnaround time?

Before committing

  • Photograph the original fretwork and instrument condition.
  • Record desired action/relief and measure the fret size if possible.
  • Ask whether modification affects any remaining warranty and understand resale/originality implications.
  • Keep certificate, receipt, replaced parts if meaningful, and the luthier invoice.

Practical recommendation

If the guitar is truly near-mint, play and maintain it. Reassess when wear becomes measurable or after you have enough time with it to know exactly which stainless wire/profile you want. A superb original nickel fret job is better than a mediocre stainless refret.
