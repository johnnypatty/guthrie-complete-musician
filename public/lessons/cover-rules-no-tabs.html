Self-Made Cover Rules — No Tabs First

Your first complete version comes from the recording, your ears, and your musical decisions. A tab may later diagnose differences, but it cannot design your interpretation.

Progressive repertoire-covers • cover • rules • tabs

The promise

Your first complete version comes from the recording, your ears, and your musical decisions. A tab may later diagnose differences, but it cannot design your interpretation.

Project sequence

  1. Choose the exact version/recording and a realistic section.
  2. Write the form and timestamps by listening.
  3. Sing/map the melody, main riff, and bass roots.
  4. Identify chord qualities/functions as far as your ear allows.
  5. Transcribe in tiny loops using the No-Tabs Method.
  6. Decide fingering for sound and control—not because a video shows it.
  7. Match articulation, rhythm, dynamics, and tone before maximum speed.
  8. Make one personal arrangement decision: register, harmony, rhythm, intro/outro, instrumentation, or improvised section.
  9. Record a full take without stopping.
  10. Only then verify uncertain details and annotate what your ear missed.

What you must submit to yourself

  • form/chord map;
  • transcription worksheet;
  • isolated section recording;
  • full unedited take;
  • notes on three differences between source and your version;
  • one intentional personal choice;
  • one next improvement.

Accuracy versus ownership

For iconic composed solos, accuracy may serve the song. For live/improvised sections, preserve the role, contour, harmony, and character while allowing your own phrasing. Label which goal you chose.

Common mistake

Spending weeks on the first impossible fast bar. Map the entire form at a simpler level, then return to the technical detail.
