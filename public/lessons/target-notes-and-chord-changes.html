Improvisation and Phrasing • Weeks 5-24

Target Notes and Chord Changes

Make the harmony audible through melodic arrival while preserving rhythm and phrase continuity.

Intermediate to advanced improvisation-phrasing • target • chord • changes

Purpose

Make the harmony audible through melodic arrival while preserving rhythm and phrase continuity.

Method

Level 1 — one target family

Over a progression, land only on roots at changes. Then repeat with thirds, sevenths, and one chosen extension. Approach from silence first.

Level 2 — nearest destination

Before each change, find the closest 3rd or 7th of the next chord. Play one approach note and arrive on a strong subdivision. Use common tones when they create continuity.

Level 3 — approach vocabulary

Approach a target by:

  • scale step below/above;
  • chromatic below/above;
  • enclosure (one side then the other);
  • arpeggio connection;
  • bend or slide into pitch.

The approach is permitted to sound tense; the arrival must be intentional.

Level 4 — delayed resolution

Sometimes cross the chord boundary with a held/common/suspension note, then resolve later. Compare its emotional effect to landing exactly on beat one.

Singing rule

Pause before each chorus and sing the target sequence over the backing. If you cannot hear the destinations, more scale shapes will not solve it.

Success test

Record one chorus with accompaniment and one without. Chord movement remains partly recognizable from the unaccompanied line, yet the phrase does not sound like roots/thirds played as an exercise.

Common mistake

Targeting every beat-one root so predictably that melody disappears. Use targets as destinations, not a compulsory roll call.
