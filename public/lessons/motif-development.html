Improvisation and Phrasing • Weeks 5-24

Motif Development

A motif is a small recognizable identity—often rhythm plus contour, not a specific lick.

Intermediate to advanced improvisation-phrasing • motif • development

A motif is a small recognizable identity—often rhythm plus contour, not a specific lick.

Purpose

Create coherence and learn to generate music from limited material.

Method: eight transformations

Make a two-to-five-note motif, sing it, then try:

  1. exact repetition;
  2. rhythmic displacement;
  3. rhythmic expansion or compression;
  4. sequence from another scale/chord degree;
  5. change the ending only;
  6. inversion of contour;
  7. register/articulation change;
  8. fragmentation—use only its strongest cell.

Four-chorus challenge

  • Chorus 1: state motif clearly.
  • Chorus 2: make rhythmic variations.
  • Chorus 3: follow chord changes while retaining identity.
  • Chorus 4: intensify, then return to the original.

Listener test

Play the recording for someone or listen the next day. Can the original motif be recognized after variations? If not, change fewer dimensions at once.

Success test

Create a two-minute solo largely from one motif without sounding like a loop. At least three transformations remain audible.

Common mistake

Abandoning an idea after one statement because the hands know more shapes.
