Guthrie Study Map

Study Guthrie as a problem-solver. Learn small sections, identify the principle, then make it yours.

Progressive repertoire-covers • guthrie • study

Study Guthrie as a problem-solver. Learn small sections, identify the principle, then make it yours.

Wonderful Slippery Thing

  • Hear first: sixteenth-note grid, ghosted attacks, dynamic filter response, chord/riff punctuation.
  • Technical focus: pressure-release muting, consistent attack, rhythm/lead exchange.
  • Transform: write a new two-chord funk groove and use the same envelope-controlled articulation.

Fives

  • Hear first: large pulse and grouping before pitches.
  • Technical focus: accents that travel across a repeated mechanical unit; composed versus improvised material.
  • Transform: create an original 5-based motif, then phrase it over ordinary 4/4 without losing the bar.

Waves

  • Hear first: vocal contour, long/short note balance, smooth interval connections.
  • Technical focus: legato clarity, slides/bends, dynamics, register.
  • Transform: sing an eight-note melody, then choose mixed articulations only after the melody works.

Ner Ner

  • Hear first: pocket, blues/fusion conversation, chromatic notes resolving into strong targets.
  • Technical focus: groove while improvising; inside/outside tension; phrase endings.
  • Transform: over a dominant/funk vamp, approach the 3rd or b7 chromatically and leave a full beat of space.

Erotic Cakes

  • Hear first: form and recurring ideas—not only dense lead passages.
  • Technical focus: changing articulation, sequences, clean transitions, thematic recall.
  • Transform: write a short etude with a memorable theme, a contrasting technical episode, and a return.

The four-pass study method

  1. Ear pass: sing and map 1–4 bars with no tab.
  2. Craft pass: identify rhythm, harmony, contour, articulation, dynamics, and muting.
  3. Transfer pass: transpose, change rhythm, and use over another chord.
  4. Voice pass: record a new phrase where the original source is no longer obvious.

Important

Official notation/backings can be valuable after the ear pass. Do not attempt to absorb five Guthrie songs at once. One deeply understood phrase per week is enough when it enters your improvisation.
