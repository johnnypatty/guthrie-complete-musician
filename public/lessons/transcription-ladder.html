Ear Training and Transcription • Weeks 1-24

Transcription Ladder

Move up when the process is reliable, not when one lucky song works.

Progressive ear-training • transcription • ladder

Move up when the process is reliable, not when one lucky song works.

Level 1 — Melody fragments

  • 3–8 seconds, one clear instrument, slow/moderate tempo.
  • Deliverable: sing, play in two areas, note/degree map, one variation.
  • Suggested pool: famous melodies, Fallen Down, Godfather Theme, simple vocal hooks from songs you know deeply.

Level 2 — Riffs and bass

  • 1–2 bars with rests, muting, repeated rhythm, or bass movement.
  • Deliverable: counted rhythm, root/interval map, articulation notes, one-minute groove.
  • Suggested pool: Redbone, Enter Sandman, Sad But True, My Own Summer, Pantera riffs, E1M1.

Level 3 — Expressive phrases

  • 5–15 seconds containing bends, slides, vibrato, and chord-tone landings.
  • Deliverable: bend targets, articulation timeline, backing chord, three response phrases.
  • Suggested pool: Gary Moore, Jeff Beck, Andy Timmons, Pink Floyd, Slash.

Level 4 — Harmony and connected sections

  • 15–30 seconds; melody plus bass/chord map or several linked lead phrases.
  • Deliverable: form, bass roots, chord qualities, melody degrees, complete unedited take.
  • Suggested pool: Tadow, Show Me How, Wonderful Slippery Thing groove, Cry for You, Tender Surrender.

Level 5 — Dense/fast/odd material

  • Begin with only 1–4 seconds; tempo may be reduced without pitch alteration.
  • Deliverable: rhythm grouping before notes, clean slow version, controlled tempo ladder, theoretical explanation, original transformation.
  • Suggested pool: Fives, Waves, Cliffs of Dover, Technical Difficulties, Perpetual Burn, Far Beyond the Sun, selected Necrophagist/BTBAM material.

Readiness test

Advance when you can complete three different targets at the level without tab, each with at least 80% pitch/rhythm confidence before verification. If one dimension fails repeatedly, stay at the level but change genre/instrument.

Diversity rule

For every three guitar transcriptions, include one vocal, bass, keyboard, horn, orchestral, game, or environmental melody. Guthrie-like freedom comes partly from not hearing everything as a guitar shape.
