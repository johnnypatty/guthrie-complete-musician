Extensions

Extensions work because a chord may continue stacking thirds: 9, 11, 13. On guitar, choose the tones the ear needs.

  • maj9: major 7 color plus 9.
  • m9: minor 7 color plus natural 9.
  • 9: dominant 7 plus 9.
  • 13: dominant family with the 13 color; often omit 5 and sometimes 9/11.
  • add9: triad plus 9, without implying a seventh.

Alterations

On dominant chords, b9, #9, #11/b5, and b13/#5 increase tension toward a destination. The altered tone must be heard as movement, not displayed as vocabulary.

Example: A7 resolving to Dm. Essential A7 tones are C# (3) and G (b7). Add Bb (b9) or F (#5/b13), then resolve each by a small melodic motion into Dm tones.

Omission hierarchy

In a band/backing context:

  1. Bass may supply root.
  2. Natural fifth often adds little identity.
  3. Third and seventh define family/function.
  4. One or two extensions/alterations define color.
  5. Top note controls the melodic impression.

Weekly exercise

Choose a four-chord progression. Make plain seventh, colorful, and sparse band-friendly versions. Record with the offline backing track and decide which register avoids fighting the bass/pad.

Success test

You can explain every note in a voicing and hear an extension/alteration resolve. You can simplify a dense symbol to its essential guitar part.
