Clean Speed System

Speed is the result of efficient motion, accurate timing, reliable muting, and gradual exposure—not maximum effort.

Progressive technique • clean • speed • system

Speed is the result of efficient motion, accurate timing, reliable muting, and gradual exposure—not maximum effort.

Purpose

Discover your current clean zone, improve it without hiding errors, and transfer faster motion into real phrases.

Method: the five-zone session

Choose a 1–2 second original cell that exposes one mechanic. Record the frets/notes and subdivision so the test remains comparable.

  1. Map (50–60%): play slowly enough to watch pick path, finger release, string changes, and muting.
  2. Control (70–80%): five repetitions with identical rhythm and dynamics.
  3. Burst (90–110%): one beat fast, then rest for one or more beats. Use 6–10 attempts, never a continuous struggle.
  4. Bridge: two beats fast, two beats easy; then one full bar.
  5. Music: place the motion in a two-bar phrase with a target note, accent, rest, and dynamic shape.

Tempo rule

  • Start where two complete takes are unquestionably clean.
  • Raise by 2–4 BPM only after two clean takes with tension at 3/10 or lower.
  • If two attempts fail in the same way, lower 5–10% and diagnose.
  • Stop the speed portion after three quality drops; switch to slow mapping or music.
  • Test the top clean tempo only twice weekly.

Diagnosis matrix

SymptomLikely questionNext experiment
First note lateIs the pick already prepared?Silent setup, breathe, start on click
String change noisyWhich hand owns the mute?Play only the crossing and adjacent rests
Ascending works, descending failsPick escape/finger release differsSeparate directions, film close-up
Timing compressesSubdivision is not internalAccent every group, then remove accent
Forearm locksTempo/duration too highOne-beat bursts, lighter grip, longer rest
Notes blur under gainReleases/muting unclearUse clean tone, then restore gain

Success test

Two recorded full repetitions with even subdivision, clear attacks, silent unused strings, intended accents, and tension no higher than 3/10. The same motion must survive in a short musical phrase.

Common mistake

Practising the mistake at high speed until it feels familiar. Familiar is not efficient.
