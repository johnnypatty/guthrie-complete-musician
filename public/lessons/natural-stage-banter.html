Natural Stage Banter

Being funny like Guthrie does not mean copying Guthrie's jokes. His strongest lesson is comfort, observation, timing, vocabulary, and treating the audience like intelligent humans.

All levels personality-humour • natural • stage • banter

Being funny like Guthrie does not mean copying Guthrie's jokes. His strongest lesson is comfort, observation, timing, vocabulary, and treating the audience like intelligent humans.

The 20-second introduction

Use three beats:

  1. Context: one thing the audience needs or might enjoy knowing.
  2. Personal detail/angle: why it matters to you, preferably specific.
  3. Launch: a clean final sentence that leads into the music.

Do not explain the entire composition or apologize before playing.

Humour tools

  • Observation: notice a real contradiction or tiny detail.
  • Rule of three: establish two related items, make the third surprising.
  • Misdirection: create a reasonable expectation, then reveal a different honest meaning.
  • Callback: refer later to something that happened earlier in the set.
  • Understatement: describe something extreme calmly.
  • Pause: let the audience understand; do not step on the response.

Recovery

If a line fails, smile, continue, and do not explain why it should have worked. The audience takes emotional instructions from your reaction.

Boundaries

Never humiliate audience members, bandmates, or vulnerable groups. Avoid forced self-hatred. Warmth and curiosity age better than shock.

Weekly exercise

Record a 30–60 second introduction to that week's piece. Watch once muted for body language, listen once without video for clarity/timing, then normally. Keep the idea that sounded most natural.

Success test

The introduction is concise, understandable, sounds like your ordinary voice at its clearest, and helps the audience want to hear the music.
