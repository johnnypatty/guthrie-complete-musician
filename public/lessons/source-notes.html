All levels learning-resources • source

Checked on 2026-08-08. These are the sources used to design this course. “Free” means the linked material can be read or used without purchasing it; regional prices and availability may change.

Guthrie Govan — first-party teaching and advice

Creative Guitar 1: Cutting-Edge Techniques

  • Author/publisher: Guthrie Govan; Music Sales America / Hal Leonard
  • Access: paid book; accompanying audio depends on the edition
  • Link: https://www.halleonard.com/product/14007762/creative-guitar-1
  • Used for: foundations, theory, pentatonic organization, arpeggios, and turning knowledge into music.
  • Limitation: it is a curriculum source, not a magic shortcut; the audio may be supplied on CD in older stock.

Creative Guitar 2: Advanced Techniques

  • Author/publisher: Guthrie Govan; Music Sales America / Hal Leonard
  • Access: paid book
  • Link: https://www.halleonard.com/product/14007763/creative-guitar-2
  • Used for: advanced articulation and technique after the first book's concepts are usable.
  • Limitation: advanced examples should not replace timing, ear work, or actual improvisation.

Guthrie on practice and improvisation

  • Publisher: Guitar World
  • Access: free article
  • Link: https://www.guitarworld.com/news/guthrie-govan-practice-tips
  • Used for: prioritizing the weakest link and treating improvisation as a real skill practised regularly.
  • Limitation: editorial summary rather than a complete progressive course.

Theory and ear training

Open Music Theory: triads and seventh chords

  • Publisher: Open Music Theory
  • Access: free/open textbook
  • Link: https://openmusictheory.github.io/triads.html
  • Used for: chord members, qualities, inversions, Roman numerals, and note spelling.
  • Limitation: examples are not guitar-specific; this course converts the concepts to string sets and voice leading.

Open Music Theory: pop/rock harmony

  • Publisher: Open Music Theory
  • Access: free/open textbook
  • Link: https://openmusictheory.github.io/popRockHarmony.html
  • Used for: analyzing rock progressions without forcing every song into classical syntax.
  • Limitation: it is a reference, not a listening substitute.

Open Music Theory: melodic voice leading

  • Publisher: Open Music Theory
  • Access: free/open textbook
  • Link: https://openmusictheory.github.io/melodicKeyboardStyle.html
  • Used for: nearest-note motion, singable top lines, contrary/oblique motion, and smooth chord connections.
  • Limitation: strict-style rules become creative options in rock/fusion rather than absolute laws.

musictheory.net exercises

  • Publisher: musictheory.net
  • Access: free web exercises; optional paid Tenuto app for offline iOS use
  • Link: https://www.musictheory.net/exercises
  • Used for: interval, scale, chord, note, and key-signature identification/construction.
  • Limitation: visual exercises must be paired with singing, hearing, and finding the answer on guitar.

Practice quality and physical sustainability

Interleaved practice in advanced musical performance

  • Citation: Carter and Grahn, Frontiers in Psychology (2016)
  • Access: free abstract/full-text links
  • Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27588014/
  • Used for: alternating related tasks and revisiting them instead of relying only on long blocks of identical repetition.
  • Limitation: one study does not prove one universal schedule; this course combines interleaving with focused blocks.

Distribution of practice and motor-skill retention

  • Citation: Savion-Lemieux and Penhune, Experimental Brain Research (2005)
  • Access: abstract
  • Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15551084/
  • Used for: consistency across days rather than attempting to replace a week with one enormous session.
  • Limitation: laboratory motor sequences are simpler than complete guitar performance.

Imagine, Sing, Play

  • Citation: Bernardi et al., open-access research article
  • Access: free full text
  • Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8573425/
  • Used for: combining mental, vocal, and physical practice; hearing an idea before the fingers execute it.
  • Limitation: results do not mean physical instrument practice can be omitted.

Performance-related musculoskeletal disorders in musicians

  • Citation: Chan and Ackermann, open-access clinical review
  • Access: free full text
  • Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4086404/
  • Used for: gradual playing-load changes and 10–15 minute breaks after 45–60 minutes on long sessions.
  • Limitation: preventive education, not personal diagnosis or treatment.

Berklee practice materials

  • Publisher: Berklee PULSE
  • Access: free educational PDF
  • Link: https://pulse.berklee.edu/content/public/pubunitassets/CasioVideoSeries/video2/Lesson2practicing_technique.pdf
  • Used for: daily tracking, warming up, lowering tempo for accuracy, and metronome use.
  • Limitation: general musicianship material rather than a complete advanced-guitar program.

Communication, humour, and storytelling

Toastmasters public-speaking tips

  • Publisher: Toastmasters International
  • Access: free
  • Link: https://www.toastmasters.org/en-us/resources/public-speaking-tips
  • Used for: audience awareness, preparation, delivery, and confidence.
  • Limitation: public speaking is not identical to stage banter; exercises are shortened for gigs and videos.

Storytelling tips from contest winners

  • Publisher: Toastmasters International
  • Access: free
  • Link: https://www.toastmasters.org/Magazine/Magazine%20Issues/2023/Sept/Storytelling%20Tips
  • Used for: clear personal stories, removing ego, and making details serve the audience.
  • Limitation: competition speeches are longer and more formal than musical introductions.

Your Comedy Checklist

  • Publisher: Toastmasters International
  • Access: free
  • Link: https://www.toastmasters.org/en-us/magazine/articles/your-comedy-checklist
  • Used for: delivery, phrasing, facial expression, and observing real contradictions rather than copying jokes.
  • Limitation: humour remains personal and context-dependent; laughter is not the only success measure.
